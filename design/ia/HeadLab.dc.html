A big rise means the model relied on these heads. Little or no change means they weren’t doing much on this text, or other heads covered for them. Models can compensate, so no change doesn’t prove a head is useless. Switch a head off to see how the answer and the error change without it. Switch several off to see whether they cover for each other.
Switching off replaces the head’s output with its average over held-back text, so the rest of the model still gets a normal-sized signal. Setting it to zero instead is harsher; the engine can do both. Ranking by importance switches each head off on its own and measures how much the error rises. Heads are ranked by that rise. The three tests measure attention on prepared text: share on the previous word, share on the first word (an idle-head sign), and, for induction, on random sequences repeated twice, share on the word after the earlier copy of the current word.
